# Persona: The Founding Curator

**Slug:** social-loop-founding-curator
**Priority:** Primary
**Date:** 2026-06-06

## Who they are

A working member of the writing world: an author, an editor, a small-press person, or a serious book friend of the founder. They read widely and hold opinions worth trusting. They already curate informally, in newsletters, group chats, and recommendations to peers, where that taste evaporates into feeds and disappears. They are an early adopter here because a friend asked them, not because they went looking for another book app. Their comfort with new tools is enough to get started when a friend walks them in.

Their motivation starts relational. They want to help the founder seed the platform. The decisive fact about them: that goodwill is a loan, not a foundation. They keep going only if the app earns a place in their own reading life and makes their taste visible to the people they care about.

## What they want

Their taste recognized and carried, so recommending a book becomes durable curation instead of a disposable post, while they discover what their peers are actually reading.

## Their core loop

Rate, tag, and review books they know → see their taste reflected and shaping a book's consensus → discover what trusted peers value through taste match and hidden gems → vouch for other good curators to bring them in → the trust graph deepens → discovery gets better for everyone, including them.

## What they won't tolerate

- Unpaid data-entry that benefits only the platform.
- Ratings that echo into an empty room.
- Being treated as a beta tester rather than a respected voice.
- Effort that cannot travel with them (lock-in, non-portable credit).

## Notes

This persona is the seed of the trust graph and the bridge to every other user. Their vouching (tagging peers as curators) is the growth mechanism that lets the graph expand past the operator-seeded librarian follow list. Their shared links are how the Trusting Reader arrives.

The central design risk is the conversion from favor to self-interest. The experience must demonstrate personal value within the first session or two, or the relational goodwill runs out before the graph is dense enough to pay off on its own. This collides directly with the cold-start reality: early on, their ratings have few peers to match against, so the very payoff that would hook them is the thinnest. Scope and experience phases must confront this honesty-versus-liveliness tension head on.
