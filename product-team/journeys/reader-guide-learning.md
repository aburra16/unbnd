# Journey: Learning Unbnd — the guide's four entry moments

**Slug:** reader-guide-learning
**Personas:** reuses `product-team/personas/social-loop-{trusting-reader,founding-curator,sovereignty-curious}.md` (no new personas; this epic sees them in their *learning* moments)
**Date:** 2026-06-11

One journeys document for the epic. The guide is not a destination people browse for pleasure; it is entered at four distinct moments, and each moment wants a different door. The structure that serves all four is the epic's central design question (discovery open questions 1–3).

## Moment A: "What is this place?" — the newcomer's first ten minutes
**Persona:** the Trusting Reader, freshly poached from Goodreads.
- **Trigger:** they made an account (or are deciding to) and want to know what they are signing up for. Some arrive before their first action; many arrive after a confusing first glance at a book page.
- **Action:** they look for something like "how this works" or "getting started." Today there is nothing; they pattern-match from Goodreads and silently misread the trust layer.
- **Expected experience:** a short, warm, plainly written walkthrough of the first session: make an account, rate a few books, shelve, follow someone, and one honest paragraph on why this place is different (people you trust, instead of a crowd average) with zero protocol vocabulary. Readable in minutes, not an encyclopedia.
- **Emotional state:** curious, low commitment, allergic to homework. If the first screen of the guide feels like a manual, they close it.

## Moment B: "What does THIS mean?" — confused in the moment
**Persona:** any signed-in reader, mid-task.
- **Trigger:** a specific unexplained surface: the House/Yours toggle, a taste-match percentage, "Hidden gem," a vouch count, a struck-through "contested" tag, "Removal queued."
- **Action:** today they either guess, ask the founder, or ignore the feature forever. The third outcome is the silent killer: the trust layer becomes decoration.
- **Expected experience:** a direct answer per feature, findable in seconds: what it is, what it is based on, what they can do with it, each with a concrete walkthrough ("to see books your trusted people loved that the crowd missed, do this"). Whether the app itself links to these answers from the point of confusion is the contextual-entry-points scope question.
- **Emotional state:** mildly annoyed, in the middle of something. Wants one paragraph, not a chapter.

## Moment C: the recruit's on-ramp
**Persona:** the Founding Curator, day one.
- **Trigger:** the founder's ask now includes a link: "here's the five-minute version of what this is and why your ratings matter."
- **Action:** they read before or right after their first session; the guide replaces the live walkthrough the founder gives today.
- **Expected experience:** everything in Moment A, plus the curator layer in plain words: what it means that their taste carries weight, what vouching is, what the curator tools (submitting books, promoting, flagging) do. The dignity test: it reads like an explanation written for a respected colleague, never like onboarding copy for a "beta tester."
- **Emotional state:** doing a favor, deciding whether this respects their time. The guide is part of how the product earns the second session.

## Moment D: the sovereignty decision
**Persona:** the Sovereignty-Curious User.
- **Trigger:** the "Take ownership of your account" card, or a nostr-adjacent friend's question ("can you take your account with you?").
- **Action:** they want the full, calm explanation before touching a flow that reveals a secret key: what they have now, what changes, what stays the same, what they are responsible for afterward.
- **Expected experience:** the one place in the product where the bigger idea (your account is portable; your words are yours) is explained completely, still without requiring the reader to learn protocol vocabulary to make the decision. The in-app four-step flow stays short; the guide carries the depth.
- **Emotional state:** cautious. Trust here is won by completeness and calm, lost by either hand-waving or jargon.

## What the moments imply (for scope and experience)
1. Two reading shapes, one guide: a short narrative path (A and C read it start to finish) and a per-feature reference (B dips in; D reads one deep entry). C is A plus the curator section, which suggests one narrative with a clearly marked curator extension rather than two documents.
2. Findability beats completeness at the moment of confusion (B): names of entries must match the words on screen ("House view," "Taste match," "Hidden gems"), so a confused reader can find the answer by the label that confused them.
3. The voice is one register everywhere: plain, warm, unhurried, jargon-free, and human-sounding per the tic taxonomy. The reader should never be able to tell where one entry's author ended and another began.
